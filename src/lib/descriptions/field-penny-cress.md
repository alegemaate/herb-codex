Field pennycress is a common weed of grain fields and roadsides. Crush it and you'll understand its other name, "stinkweed": it smells strongly of garlic.

Its seed pods are the best part. They're round, flat and papery, like little coins, and they shine in the sun as they dry.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** on the stem are long and toothed, and they clasp the stem with two small ear-like lobes.
- **Flowers** are small and white, with four petals, in a cluster at the top of the stem.
- **Seed pods** are round and flat, about 1 cm across, with a notch at the top.
- **The smell** is garlicky when you crush any part of the plant.

## What it's good for

Farmers are starting to take this weed seriously. Plant breeders are developing pennycress as a winter cover crop, because it protects the soil over winter and its oily seeds can be made into biofuel.

The young leaves are edible but very bitter, so few people eat them. Some of its close relatives can absorb heavy metals from polluted soil, and scientists study them as a way to clean up contaminated land.
