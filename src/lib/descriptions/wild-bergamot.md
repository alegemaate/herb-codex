Wild bergamot is a native wildflower of North American meadows and prairies, and one of the best plants you can grow for bees. In midsummer it's covered in shaggy lavender pompoms that bees, butterflies and hummingbirds visit all day.

It's also called bee balm. Despite the name, it's not related to the bergamot orange that flavours Earl Grey tea, though the leaves smell a little like it.

## How to spot it

- **Flowers** are lavender to pale purple, in round, shaggy heads at the top of the stem. Each head is made of many thin, tube-shaped flowers.
- **Leaves** are opposite, lance-shaped and toothed, and often greyish-green.
- **Stems** are square, like the rest of the mint family.
- **The smell** is strong, like oregano and thyme, when you crush a leaf.

## What it's good for

The leaves and flowers make a spicy, oregano-like seasoning and a strong herbal tea. Many Indigenous peoples used it for colds, sore throats and fevers. It contains thymol, the same antiseptic oil found in thyme.

It's a wonderful garden plant for pollinators, and it spreads slowly into a clump.

## Don't confuse it with

Scarlet bee balm, or Oswego tea, is a close relative with bright red flowers. It grows in wetter places and is used the same way.
