Wild strawberries are tiny, often no bigger than a fingernail, but they have far more flavour than anything from the shop. Finding a patch of ripe ones in June is one of the small joys of early summer.

The big garden strawberry is actually a cross between this North American species and a wild strawberry from Chile.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** have three toothed, oval leaflets on a long, hairy stalk.
- **Flowers** are white with five round petals and a yellow centre. They usually sit among or just below the leaves.
- **Runners** spread out along the ground and root to make new plants, so it often forms large patches.
- **Berries** are small and red, with the seeds sitting in tiny pits on the surface.

## What it's good for

The berries, of course. Eat them fresh on the spot, because they don't keep. If you collect enough, they make an outstanding jam.

The leaves make a pleasant, mild tea. Use them fresh or fully dried. Herbalists use the leaf tea as a gentle remedy for an upset stomach.

Bees visit the flowers, and birds, turtles and small mammals eat the berries.

## Don't confuse it with

- **Mock strawberry** has yellow flowers and a bland, dry berry with the seeds sitting on top instead of in pits. It's not dangerous, just disappointing.
- **Woodland strawberry** has its flowers and berries held above the leaves, and longer, more cone-shaped fruit. It's edible too.
