In early summer, bird vetch turns field edges and roadsides purple. It's a climbing member of the pea family, and it scrambles over grass and shrubs using little tendrils at the ends of its leaves.

It came over from Europe and Asia. In some places it's welcome for feeding bees and enriching the soil. In others it smothers everything around it.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** are made of 8 to 12 pairs of narrow leaflets, and each leaf ends in a curling tendril.
- **Flowers** are violet-blue and pea-shaped, all lined up along one side of a long stalk like a comb.
- **Pods** are flat and turn brown when the seeds are ripe.
- **Height** is 60–120 cm when it has something to climb, or it sprawls in a tangle when it doesn't.

## What it's good for

Bees and butterflies love it. Like other legumes, it carries bacteria in its roots that pull nitrogen from the air and feed it into the soil.

It isn't a plant for the kitchen. Some vetch seeds contain toxic compounds, and there's no tradition of eating this one.
