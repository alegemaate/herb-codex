If your lawn has a creeping carpet of round, scalloped leaves that smells like mint when you mow it, you've met ground ivy. Gardeners call it creeping Charlie and mostly want it gone. It spreads by runners that root at every joint, so pulling it out is a slow job.

Before hops took over, brewers used it to flavour and clear their ale. That's where the old name "alehoof" comes from.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** are round to kidney-shaped, with soft, rounded teeth, and grow in pairs.
- **Stems** are square, like every member of the mint family, and creep along the ground.
- **The smell** is the giveaway: crush a leaf and you get a strong, slightly bitter mint scent.
- **Flowers** are small, blue-violet funnels tucked in the leaf joints in spring.

## What it's good for

The leaves are edible but strong, so a little goes a long way. Add a few to soups or salads, or steep them for a herbal tea.

Herbalists have long used ground ivy tea for coughs, colds and stuffy sinuses, and they put crushed leaves on small cuts. Not much modern research backs this up, but it has a long history of use.

## Don't confuse it with

Henbit and purple dead-nettle grow in the same spots and also have square stems and purple flowers. They grow upright instead of creeping, and they don't have that minty smell.
