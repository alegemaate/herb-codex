Catsear, also called false dandelion, looks enough like a dandelion that most people never notice the difference. The two are close relatives, and both are edible, so mixing them up isn't a problem in the kitchen.

It loves lawns, pastures and roadsides, and it often keeps flowering long after the dandelions have finished. It will even grow through cracks in concrete.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** grow in a flat rosette, with shallow, rounded lobes. They're covered in stiff hairs, a bit like a cat's ear.
- **Flower stalks** are tall, solid and wiry, and they often branch, with a flower at each tip.
- **Flowers** are yellow and look like small dandelions.
- **Seed heads** are fluffy white clocks, just like a dandelion's.

## What it's good for

Use it the same way as dandelion: leaves in salads or cooked, flowers as a garnish, and the root roasted. The leaves are less bitter than dandelion, but the hairs make them a little rough when raw, so many people prefer them cooked.

## Telling it from dandelion

- **Leaves:** dandelion leaves are smooth; catsear leaves are hairy.
- **Stalks:** dandelion stalks are hollow and carry one flower each; catsear stalks are solid and often branched, with several flowers.
