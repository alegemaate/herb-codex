Orange hawkweed is hard to miss. Its flowers are a bright, burnt orange that few other wildflowers come close to, which earned it the name "devil's paintbrush". It brightens fields, roadsides and lawns from early summer.

It came from the mountains of Europe as a garden flower and escaped. It spreads by runners and seeds and can take over pastures, so in some places it's listed as a noxious weed.

## How to spot it

- **Flowers** are bright orange to red-orange, with notched petal tips, in a tight cluster at the top of the stalk. Unopened buds are dark red.
- **The stalk** is leafless and covered in dark, bristly hairs.
- **Leaves** are long, hairy and spoon-shaped, in a rosette at the base.
- **Sap** is white and milky if you break the stalk.

## What it's good for

Not much for people. It's not a food plant, and it has little history as a medicine. Bees, butterflies and hoverflies do visit the flowers.

## Don't confuse it with

Yellow hawkweeds look very similar but have yellow flowers. They're close relatives and just as weedy.
