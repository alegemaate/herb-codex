Annual fleabane is a native North American wildflower that fills fields and roadsides with tiny white daisies from late spring until autumn. Each flower is only about the size of a fingernail, but a single plant can carry dozens of them.

The name comes from an old belief that bunches of the dried plant would drive fleas out of a house.

## How to spot it

- **Flowers** are small daisies with 40 or more very thin white rays, sometimes tinged pink, around a yellow centre.
- **Leaves** on the stem are lance-shaped and toothed. The lower leaves are wider and have long stalks.
- **Stems** are hairy and branch near the top, growing 30–90 cm tall.

## What it's good for

Mostly for the insects. Small bees, hoverflies and butterflies visit it all summer. Some Indigenous peoples used it to treat skin problems and as a diuretic, but there's little research on those uses.

## Don't confuse it with

- **Philadelphia fleabane** flowers earlier, in spring. It has even more, finer rays, and its leaves wrap around the stem.
- **Oxeye daisy** has much bigger flowers, 3–5 cm across, with fewer, wider white rays.
