Bull thistle is the big, fierce thistle of pastures and roadsides. Every part of it seems to be armed with spines, and cattle leave it alone, which is why it can take over a field.

It takes two years to grow. In the first year it lies flat in a spiny rosette. In the second it grows a tall stalk topped with purple flowers.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** are deeply lobed, and each lobe ends in a long, sharp spine. The top of the leaf is rough and prickly, like sandpaper.
- **Stems** have spiny wings running down them from the base of each leaf.
- **Flowers** are big purple tufts, 3–5 cm across, sitting on a spiny, rounded base.
- **Seeds** float away on feathery white down.

## What it's good for

If you're brave enough, thistle is edible. Cut the young stalk of a second-year plant before it flowers and peel away every spine. The inside tastes like celery crossed with artichoke. First-year roots can be cooked, and the leaf midribs can be eaten once the spines are trimmed off.

It's also great for wildlife. Bees and butterflies love the flowers, and goldfinches eat the seeds and line their nests with the down.

## Other thistles

- **Canada thistle** has smaller, pale purple flowers in clusters and spreads by its roots. Its stems have no spiny wings.
- **Milk thistle** is a different plant, with white-marbled leaves. It's the thistle that herbal liver remedies are made from, not this one.
