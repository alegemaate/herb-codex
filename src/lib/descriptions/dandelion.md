Dandelion might be the most familiar wild plant there is, and every part of it is edible. The name comes from the French _dent de lion_, "lion's tooth", after the jagged edges of its leaves.

Most people see it as a lawn weed. For bees, it's one of the first good meals of spring.

## How to spot it

- **Leaves** all come from a rosette at ground level. They're smooth and hairless, with deep teeth that point back towards the centre.
- **Flower stalks** are smooth, hollow and leafless, with one flower each. Break one and white, milky sap comes out.
- **Flowers** are bright yellow and made of many small petals.
- **Seed heads** are the round, white "clocks" every child has blown on.
- **The root** is a thick, brown taproot that snaps if you don't dig carefully.

## What it's good for

Pick young leaves in early spring for salads, before they turn bitter. Older leaves are better cooked. The flowers make fritters, syrup and dandelion wine. Roast and grind the root and you get a caffeine-free drink that tastes a bit like coffee.

The leaves are rich in vitamins A, C and K and in potassium. Herbalists use them as a diuretic and use the root as a bitter tonic for digestion and the liver.

## Don't confuse it with

Catsear (false dandelion) looks very similar and is also edible. Dandelion leaves are smooth; catsear leaves are hairy. Dandelion stalks are hollow with one flower; catsear stalks are solid, wiry and often branched.
