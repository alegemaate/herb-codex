Motherwort is a tall, rangy member of the mint family that grows along fences, at field edges and in neglected corners of farms. In summer it's covered in little pink flowers and buzzing with bees.

Its Latin name says a lot about its history. _Leonurus_ means "lion's tail", after the shape of its flower spikes, and _cardiaca_ refers to its use as a herb for the heart.

## How to spot it

- **Lower leaves** are deeply cut into three to five pointed lobes, a bit like a hand or a maple leaf. Leaves higher up are narrower, with three points.
- **Stems** are square, often purplish, and grow 60–150 cm tall.
- **Flowers** are small, furry and pink to pale purple. They grow in rings around the stem at each pair of upper leaves.
- **Seed heads** have sharp, spiny tips. Wear gloves if you handle the plant in late summer.

## What it's good for

Motherwort has been a herbalist's favourite for centuries. A bitter tea or tincture of the flowering tops is a traditional remedy for anxiety, a racing heart and period pain. As the name suggests, it's especially known as a herb for women's health.

Modern research is limited. The bees, though, have no doubts about it.
